# INTRUDER: the story

This is canon. Every line in the game should agree with it. Written from what the game actually does, so each story beat is something the player does or sees.

**In one sentence:** the humans burned your world and shipped your whole species away. You went after them, they caught you and experimented on you, and you got out by being exactly what they were testing for. You go home to see what is left, and home tells you where your people are: on the road to Earth. So that is where home is now.

**The theme: vengeance at all costs.** You never doubt and you never turn back. The costs are real, and you pay every one of them without flinching. This is not a story where you learn that revenge is wrong.

## The thread the player always holds

The tracker has two lines: why, and what next. Both are driven by src/game/campaign.js.

| Beat | Why | What it becomes |
|---|---|---|
| The chase | GET THE ELDER BACK | Caught: gas, then the trial chamber |
| The trial and the deck | ESCAPE THE HALCYON. GET HOME. | Touch the chart: now you know the way, and the ship knows you know |
| Open space | GET HOME | The wall of hands: no one is here, every hand is warm, they are all alive |
| The road | CROSS TO EARTH. FIND OUR PEOPLE. | Fuel, plating, coolant, heading: each from the people Earth has hurt |

"Home" is never dropped. It changes meaning at the wall: not a place any more, but wherever they are.

## The goal

1. **Find your people.** Forty-one thousand of them, spread through the human fleet. The elder is one of many.
2. **Free them.** Every ship you raid and every hold you open adds to your numbers.
3. **Take Earth.** They burned your world, so you take theirs. It becomes your people's new home, and the humans pay for every valley.

## The rule that holds it all together

**What we touch, we keep.** Your people write nothing down. Memory passes by touch, body to body, and it is how they stay one people. Anything you touch for long enough becomes part of you.

That one idea explains all of the gameplay:

| What the player does | Why it makes sense |
|---|---|
| Palms meet with the elder | It is how your people say everything |
| Absorb the sidearm, arm becomes a weapon | You touched it, so you keep it |
| Fly a human ship with no training | You touch the controls and you know them |
| Absorb robots, get stronger, learn forms | Same thing, and they carry memories you can read |
| Each weapon changes the arm | The body becomes what it learned |

It is also why the humans want your species alive: soldiers who can learn any weapon by holding it. And it is why they should be afraid, because forty-one thousand of you can learn every ship and every gun they have.

## Who is who

- **You.** Unnamed. Left for dead when the valley burned. The ship logs you as **Specimen 09**.
- **The elder.** An elder of your valley, one of many elders among your people, and the one who stayed with you. **Specimen 08.** Speaks once in Chapter One, through the mark on the door.
- **The others from your valley.** Your neighbours: **Specimens 01 to 07**, taken for study over the past months.
- **Your people.** Every valley on the planet was emptied. **Forty-one thousand** are alive in the fleet's holds.
- **HALCYON.** The research vessel, and the cold AI that now runs it. Its machines are the enemy you fight.
- **MERIDIAN.** The warship that burned your valley. The crew moved onto it. It joins the fleet carrying the eight.
- **LANDER.** The stolen ship's own computer. It only knows it is going home.

**The title:** the humans came into your valley uninvited. When you board their ship, their AI calls *you* the intruder. By the end of the game, you are the intruder on Earth.

## Chapter One: The Halcyon

1. **The valley.** Quiet now. The others walked out one by one and never came back. You walk to the elder and your palms meet.
2. **The attack.** The Meridian comes down, fighters strafe the valley and everything alive in it panics. A beam lifts the elder out, thrashing, while you run after it, reaching. A crippled lander falls out of the sky at you, and the blast knocks you flat.
3. **The ruins.** You come round on the ground. They took everyone. The lander that hit you is lying there. *So they can fall.* Its pilot is dead on the ground, the first human you have ever seen. You touch the sidearm and your arm becomes the Shardcaster. From it you learn their word for themselves: human.
4. **The lander.** You board. The computer says the pilot is not responding and it is flying home to the Halcyon. *Then take me there.*
5. **The flight.** The Halcyon challenges Lander Four, then sends fighters. You salvage homing barbs from the wrecks. Close in, it notices the pilot has no pulse and something else is flying. It calls off the fighters: *let it dock.* It wants you alive.
6. **The gas.** No guards, no alarms. Bay Three seals and fills with anesthetic. The last thing you hear: *Specimen Nine secured. Awaiting collection.*
   **Trial twenty-seven.** You wake behind glass. Twenty-six trials have happened while you slept: they have been feeding you their machines. The Halcyon offers you its scanner to see how much you can keep. You touch it (your first action on the ship, with the same button you absorb with for the rest of the game). It stops being theirs, the seal fails, the glass goes. *Containment breach. All units wake.* The security you fight from here on exists because of what you just did.
7. **The mark.** Out of the cell, in the bay they gassed you in. Nine. So eight went before you. On the bulkhead door is a smear you recognise: the elder's hand, dragged across it. You touch it and hear the elder: they took every valley, thousands of us are held apart in their ships, they are taking me to the hangar on deck nine. Touch everything. Keep everything. Find us. *I will. And then they will pay for every valley.*
8. **The deck.** You shoot the jammed lock and fight through the Robotics Deck to the hangar. The machines stand dormant until the alarm wakes them. The Halcyon talks to you as you go, always politely, and you answer in as few words as you can. Every machine you absorb gives up a memory, and those tell you what happened here and how big the harvest was. In a sample store you find a piece of someone you knew and take it with you (the Lash).
9. **Too late.** The hangar lift reads out: the Meridian has undocked with all eight, joining a fleet with forty-one thousand of you, bound for Earth. The Halcyon tells you nothing is left aboard for you. *Nothing but a hangar full of ships.* From outside: the warship pulls away from the Halcyon, and a stolen ship drops out of the bay and goes after it. *They burned our world. I will take theirs. Whatever it costs.*

## The chart

The room before the last fight is the chart room. Its far door will not open until you touch the chart. You learn the way home and the fleet's route, and the Halcyon learns that you know: it undocks the Meridian, sends all power to security, the rest of the deck turns red, and the last fight gets another wave. Everything after that is a consequence of that touch.

## The road

Every world can be landed on: fly into its atmosphere. On the chase they have nothing for you yet, and say so. After the Halcyon, each one holds something the crossing to Earth needs, and it is real for the ship:

- **Home**, first. The wall of hands. It opens the road.

- **Vael**, a human mining moon. **Fuel:** the ship boosts harder. Low gravity, no air, black shadows, your burning homeworld overhead. The terminal is dead until something with current in it touches it, which is what absorbing a walker teaches you.
- **Ochre**, desert world. The **Kessith**, tall robed traders. The humans took their water eleven years ago and left them a tax. **Plating:** ship damage halved. "If you burn their world, tell them who sold it to you."
- **Skarn**, ice world. The **Grolm**, squat shell-backed miners. Machines nest in their shafts; clear them and the Foreman feeds you. **Coolant:** ship guns fire faster, and ice marrow hardens you. The Foreman promises to dig under Earth.
- **Thule**, the gas giant. The **Ummari**, floating lantern people on a cloud reef. They heard your people singing in the holds as the fleet passed under them. **The heading:** follow the song. A gift that fell through their clouds teaches you the gel form.

These are the allies for Chapter Four: everyone on this road has lost something to Earth. With all four, the ship can reach the crossing point beyond the Halcyon. That is the end of what is built.

## One star

Every world is lit by the same star, seen from where that world really is in the flight scene. Ochre is close: a big hot disc through dust. Skarn and Thule are far out: a small cold point. Vael has no air: a hard white disc, black shadows, nothing to soften them but the glow of your homeworld overhead. Home is under smoke. The valley is at night, lit by the big pale moon you can see (that moon is Vael). Skarn's sky has Thule in it, because Thule is next door. Your arms take the colour of wherever you stand.

## What absorbing does

The Rule says what you touch, you keep. The game keeps to it.

- **Every machine** heals you, thickens your hide, gives up a memory. Dull plates and live seams creep up your arms as the count rises, and the ship and you both remark on it. That is the cost, and you can see it.
- **A walker** teaches ARC, and leaves current in you: dead doors and dead terminals answer to you.
- **A crawler** teaches BARB, and how to grip: you climb higher.
- **A drone** teaches GEL, and how to drift: hold jump to fall slowly.
- **Specimen One's arm**, in the Bay Three case, gives LASH: it reaches, and pulls things to you.

## The seven

Specimens 01 to 07 each left something a machine kept in a jar, on the deck and on Vael, Skarn and Thule. Touching one gives you their voice and makes you stronger. The crew's own paperwork on the deck walls tells the same stories from the other side. A smear of the elder's hand runs from door to door along the way to the hangar: that is the trail.

## Machine memories, in order

One plays each time you absorb a machine. They persist across deaths and reset on New Game.

1. All crew transferred to the Meridian. The machines will run the Halcyon from here.
2. Survey note: they keep nothing written. Every memory passes by touch, from one body to the next.
3. Seven specimens taken from this valley for study. None of them will speak to us. None of them can.
4. Trial twenty-seven. The subject touched the scanner, and the scanner stopped being ours.
5. Command is very interested. A soldier that learns any weapon by holding it.
6. Order from the Meridian: bring the old ones first. They have kept the most.
7. Specimen Eight secured. Meridian cleared to load all specimens for transfer.
8. Harvest complete. Forty-one thousand live specimens in the fleet holds. Departing for Earth.
9. Note added by Halcyon: it is reading these. Stop letting it touch the units.

## Where it goes next (not built)

- **Chapter Two, the raids:** you leave in a stolen ship. Space-pirate loop: hunt the fleet's transports, open their holds, and each rescue grows your crew.
- **Chapter Three, the fleet of your own:** everyone you free can fly and fight, because they touch what you touched. Stolen ships become your people's fleet. You find the elder.
- **Chapter Four, Earth:** the invasion. Your people land and take it, city by city.
- **The ending:** a new home in a human city, growing over with the plants of the old valley. You got everything you came for, and you would do it all again.
- **The costs you pay:** the more machines you keep, the less of you is left. The elder asked you not to come, and will like what you become even less. You keep going anyway, because that is what "at all costs" means.

## Rules for writing

- Your thoughts are short, plain and physical. No speeches.
- You do not know human words until you have touched human things. Before the sidearm it is "they" and "their".
- The Halcyon never helps you. It is polite, and that is what makes it unpleasant.
- The elder is not gentle: "You came. I asked you not to."
- You never doubt, apologise or hesitate. No redemption arc. Other people can object to what you are doing; you do not change course.
- Only machines and the elder explain things. You just react.
